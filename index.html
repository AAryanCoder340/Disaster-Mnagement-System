3. Avoid Low-Lying Roads: Do not drive or walk through standing floodwaters or near downed electrical infrastructure.

Disaster Safety Guidelines & Response Protocols

Authoritative guidelines for coastal hazards, cyclones, storm surges, and flood management

Critical DO'S — Follow These Guidelines

  • Stay tuned to official emergency broadcasts, weather bulletins, and CoastWatch alerts.
  • Keep a sealed emergency grab-bag ready (drinking water, dry food, torch, first-aid, power bank).
  • Move to higher ground or reinforced multi-story shelters immediately if a tsunami/surge warning is issued.
  • Comply promptly with local administration and NDRF evacuation orders without delay.
  • Secure essential documents, identification, and medical records in waterproof cases.
  • Prioritize evacuation assistance for children, pregnant women, elderly, and differently-abled individuals.
  • Stay well clear of beaches, river mouths, and exposed coastal roads during high tide or cyclone advisories.

Critical DON'TS — Actions to Avoid

  • Do NOT ignore official warnings or delay evacuation when advisories are active.
  • Do NOT visit coastlines, ports, or seawalls to watch storm waves or cyclone landfall.
  • Do NOT operate electrical switches or appliances in waterlogged or flooded buildings.
  • Do NOT circulate unverified rumors, alarming social media posts, or fake distress messages.
  • Do NOT attempt to drive or wade through flooded roads or submerged bridges.
  • Do NOT leave domestic pets or livestock tethered without access to safety.
  • Do NOT return to damaged structures until cleared as safe by disaster management authorities.

Phased Disaster Management Protocols

Before Disaster
  • Map out verified primary and secondary evacuation routes
  • Formulate a family communication and reunion plan
  • Store non-perishable food and 3 days of clean water
  • Secure loose outdoor furniture, tin roofs, and fixtures
During Disaster
  • Remain indoors away from glass windows and loose facades
  • Monitor battery-operated radio and CoastWatch updates
  • Shut off main electrical breakers and LPG cylinders
  • Keep mobile phones charged and in power-saving mode
After Disaster
  • Check self and neighbors for injuries; apply first aid
  • Photograph property damage for official assessment
  • Avoid downed power lines, cracked walls, and debris
  • Report new hazards or stranded victims immediately
